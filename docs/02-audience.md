# Stone Village --- Audience

## 1. Primary audience

Affluent families who currently live in Lviv or maintain a strong
connection to the city and are considering moving from an apartment to a
larger private residential format.

The website should speak to people who are not simply searching for "a
cheap house outside the city". They are evaluating a major lifestyle
decision and compare: - premium / business-class apartment in Lviv; -
duplex near Lviv; - detached house; - competing managed cottage
communities.

## 2. Core life situation

The most useful starting tension:

**The apartment is no longer enough for the family's current life.**

Typical manifestations: - not enough personal space; - children need
separate rooms and outdoor space; - remote work competes with family
space; - no private yard or terrace; - parking is inconvenient; - desire
for more privacy; - desire to be closer to nature; - city noise and
density have become tiring; - family wants a safer and more controlled
residential environment.

## 3. Jobs to be done

### Functional

-   give each family member enough space;
-   create a separate place for work;
-   have private outdoor territory;
-   park cars next to the home;
-   remain connected to Lviv;
-   have everyday infrastructure nearby;
-   reduce the operational burden of maintaining a standalone suburban
    house.

### Emotional

-   feel that the family has moved to a higher quality of life;
-   feel calm at home;
-   feel privacy without isolation;
-   feel that children have a better everyday environment;
-   feel confidence in the scale and quality of the purchase;
-   feel that the home can adapt as the family changes.

### Social / status

-   own a substantial home rather than compromise on space;
-   live in a visually coherent, managed community;
-   choose architecture and environment that communicate taste rather
    than ostentation.

## 4. Main audience segments

### Segment A --- Upgrade from apartment

Best fit: 185 m² duplex.

Mindset: "We need more space, but I do not want to lose Lviv."

Important: - proximity to city; - practical layout; - own yard; -
office; - two cars; - family infrastructure; - understandable transition
from apartment life.

### Segment B --- Maximum family privacy

Best fit: 228 m² detached cottage.

Mindset: "If we move to a house, it should actually feel like our own
house."

Important: - detached format; - larger private territory; - privacy; -
scale; - safe room / shelter context; - covered parking; - family
zoning; - environment and services.

### Segment C --- Nature without isolation

Relevant to both products.

Mindset: "I want quiet and greenery, but not a remote village
lifestyle."

Important: - forest; - 2.5 km from Lviv; - access to city; - nearby
daily infrastructure; - managed territory.

### Segment D --- Work-from-home family

Relevant especially to 185 m².

Important: - separate office; - fibre / reliable internet if confirmed
in project assets; - workspace within the environment; - separation of
work and family life.

## 5. Decision criteria

Likely high-priority comparison criteria: 1. location and time to Lviv;
2. house format; 3. usable space; 4. land and privacy; 5. planning; 6.
actual state / readiness; 7. safety; 8. construction quality; 9.
infrastructure; 10. management and maintenance; 11. proof that the
community is real and functioning; 12. trust in developer; 13.
commercial terms.

## 6. Objections the website should resolve

-   "Will living outside Lviv become inconvenient?"
-   "Is the project actually built or mostly renders?"
-   "What infrastructure really exists?"
-   "Will children have what they need nearby?"
-   "How much maintenance will fall on me?"
-   "How private is a duplex?"
-   "What exactly is the difference between the 185 and 228 formats?"
-   "How is security handled?"
-   "What do I actually receive with the house?"
-   "Can I adapt the layout?"
-   "How does the location feel in everyday life?"
-   "Why Stone Village rather than another cottage community or a large
    apartment?"

## 7. Communication principle

Do not talk to the audience like bargain property buyers.

Avoid: - aggressive urgency; - loud luxury clichés; - "best / elite /
exclusive" without proof; - price-first communication as the main
website narrative.

Prefer: - calm confidence; - concrete evidence; - family scenarios; -
architectural and environmental quality; - clarity; - restrained premium
tone.
